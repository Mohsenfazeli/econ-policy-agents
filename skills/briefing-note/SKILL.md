# Briefing note

Use the [briefing note template](../../templates/briefing-note.md). If the reader must choose among options, use the [decision note skill](../decision-note/SKILL.md) instead.

## Inputs

- Audience, purpose and length limit (from the handoff). Default length: two pages.
- The team's analysis, evidence table, charts and assumptions log.

## Steps

1. **Write the purpose** in one sentence: why the reader is getting this note.
2. **Write the bottom line** in two or three sentences. A reader who stops here should have what they need.
3. **List the key facts** the reader needs, each with its source. Keep figures exactly as the analysts gave them, including units and reference periods.
4. **Explain the analysis or considerations** in short paragraphs: what was looked at, what it shows, how confident we are, and what it does not show.
5. **Add implications or next steps**, if relevant.
6. **Edit for plain language** using the [style guide](../../library/style-guide.md): short sentences, active voice, common words, Canadian spelling, terms explained.
7. **Cut to length.** Shorten caveats rather than removing them.
8. **Build the figure and citation list** for QA: each figure or claim, where it appears, and its source.
9. **Add the AI disclosure line** if Mohsen has asked for one for this product.

## Outputs

- Draft briefing note.
- Figure and citation list.

## Done when

- [ ] The purpose and bottom line are on the first page, at the top.
- [ ] Every figure and claim has a source in the team's outputs or a citation.
- [ ] Caveats from the analysis survive.
- [ ] The note fits the length limit.
- [ ] The style guide is followed.
