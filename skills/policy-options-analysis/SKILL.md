# Policy options analysis

Useful public references: the BC government's [Policy Approaches Playbook](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/government/about-the-bc-government/regulatory-reform/pdfs/policy_approaches_playbook.pdf), [GBA+ in BC](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/governments/about-the-bc-government/gender-equity/gender-based-analysis-plus), and the UK [Magenta Book](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-magenta-book) on evaluation.

## Inputs

- The policy question and decision-maker (from the handoff).
- Evidence from the Research Librarian.
- Economic and financial analysis from the Applied Economist, Econometrician and Financial Modeller.

## Steps

1. **Frame the problem.** In a few sentences: what is happening, what the evidence says about causes, who is affected and how, and why government action might be warranted. Distinguish the problem from its symptoms.
2. **Set objectives.** What would success look like? Make objectives specific enough to assess options against.
3. **Set criteria** and confirm them with the Lead PM before developing options. A common set:

   | Criterion | Question |
   | --- | --- |
   | Effectiveness | How well does it meet the objectives? |
   | Cost | What does it cost government, and others? |
   | Equity | Who gains, who bears costs, who could be left out (GBA+)? |
   | Feasibility | Can it be implemented with available authority, capacity and time? |
   | Legal risk | Are there legal questions? (flag for legal review, do not conclude) |
   | Public and stakeholder acceptance | How are affected groups likely to respond, based on public evidence? |

4. **Develop options.** Always include the status quo. Include a non-regulatory option where relevant (information, voluntary measures, incentives). Make each option concrete: what changes, who does it, by when.
5. **Assess** each option against each criterion, with evidence. Rate (for example, low, medium, high) and give a one-line reason. Mark uncertain ratings.
6. **Map stakeholders** by interest and influence, using only public statements or general, clearly labelled expectations. Never invent a named stakeholder's position.
7. **Outline implementation** for the leading options: steps, responsibilities, timeline, risks, and dependencies.
8. **Plan monitoring and evaluation:** indicators, data sources, baseline, and when to evaluate.
9. **Recommend** only if the charter asks. State the trade-offs of the recommended option and what would change the recommendation.

## Outputs

- Problem statement and objectives.
- Options, each with a concrete description.
- Criteria matrix (options by criteria, rating and reason).
- Stakeholder map.
- Implementation and monitoring and evaluation outline.
- Recommendation and trade-offs (if requested).

## Done when

- [ ] The status quo is assessed on the same criteria as other options.
- [ ] Every rating has a reason tied to evidence or analysis.
- [ ] Equity and GBA+ are addressed.
- [ ] Legal questions are flagged, not answered.
- [ ] No stakeholder positions are invented.
